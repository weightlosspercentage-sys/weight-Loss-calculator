Bariatric Surgery Weight Loss Calculator (%EWL & %TWL)

Medically Reviewed by Dr. Rekha Kumar, M.D., M.S. • Updated: August 14, 2026

Bariatric and metabolic surgery—including Vertical Sleeve Gastrectomy (VSG), Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (RNY), and Duodenal Switch (DS)—represents the most effective medical intervention for severe obesity. Post-operative progress in bariatric medicine is evaluated using two primary mathematical metrics: Percentage of Excess Weight Loss (%EWL) and Percentage of Total Weight Loss (%TWL).

Bariatric Formulas Explained

1. Percentage of Excess Weight Loss (%EWL)

%EWL = ((Starting Weight − Current Weight) ÷ (Starting Weight − Ideal Body Weight)) × 100

*Ideal Body Weight (IBW) is standardly defined as the weight corresponding to a healthy BMI of 25.0 kg/m² for the patient's height.

2. Percentage of Total Weight Loss (%TWL)

%TWL = ((Starting Weight − Current Weight) ÷ Starting Weight) × 100

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is a good excess weight loss percentage after surgery?

Bariatric surgeons generally define surgical success as achieving and maintaining at least 50% Excess Weight Loss (%EWL) at 1 to 2 years post-op.

Q: Why do surgeons monitor protein intake post-bariatric surgery?

Due to reduced stomach capacity and malabsorption, patients must consume 60 to 80 grams of protein daily to prevent sarcopenia (muscle loss) and preserve resting metabolic rate.
