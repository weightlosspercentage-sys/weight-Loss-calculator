BMI Calculator for Men

How to Calculate BMI — The Formula

BMI is calculated by dividing your weight by your height squared:

Metric Formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)

Example: A person weighing 70 kg and 1.75 m tall: BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75²) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9 (Healthy)

Imperial Formula

BMI = (weight in lbs ÷ height in inches²) × 703

Example: A person weighing 154 lbs and 68 inches (5'8") tall: BMI = (154 ÷ 4624) × 703 = 23.4 (Healthy)

Limitations of BMI

BMI is a useful population-level screening tool, but it has known limitations:

  • Doesn't distinguish muscle from fat — Athletes with high muscle mass are often incorrectly classified as overweight
  • Age bias — Older adults naturally lose muscle and gain fat, which BMI doesn't capture
  • Ethnic variation — Some Asian populations face metabolic risks at lower BMI thresholds (23+)
  • Doesn't show fat distribution — Abdominal fat carries higher risk than hip/thigh fat at the same BMI

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my BMI?

Use the calculator above, or manually: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). In imperial: BMI = (weight lbs ÷ height inches²) × 703. Enter your stats in the calculator above for an instant result.

What is a healthy BMI for women?

The healthy BMI range for women is 18.5 to 24.9 (WHO classification). Research suggests a BMI of 21–23 is optimal for long-term health in women. Women naturally carry more essential body fat (10–13%) than men (2–5%).

What is a healthy BMI for men?

The healthy BMI range for men is 18.5 to 24.9. A BMI of 22–24 is considered optimal for most men. Muscular men may have a BMI of 25–27 while having low body fat — in these cases, also check body fat percentage.

What BMI is considered obese?

A BMI of 30 or above is classified as obese (Class I: 30–34.9, Class II: 35–39.9, Class III: 40+). A BMI of 25–29.9 is classified as overweight. The CDC recommends consulting a healthcare provider for personalized guidance.

Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?

No — BMI cannot distinguish fat from muscle. A muscular bodybuilder may have a BMI of 28 ("overweight") despite only 8% body fat. For athletes, body fat percentage is a far more accurate health indicator.
